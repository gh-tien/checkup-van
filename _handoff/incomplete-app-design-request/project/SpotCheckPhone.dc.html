Doing the work

The app draws the vehicle, not you. A spot-check is only worth anything if nobody chose where it landed — pick from a list and the same three easy vans get walked every week while the awkward one at the back of the yard never does.

Only active vehicles go in. One that was checked in the last few days is held out so it can't come up twice in a week, and any van left too long is forced to the front until somebody walks it. Both of those numbers are yours to set, in Settings.

You can re-roll a draw a set number of times, and you can still check a named vehicle when there is a reason to — a complaint, a return from the workshop. The draw is the default, not a lock.

Suspending someone keeps every check they signed. They just can't sign in — and their open defects stay on the depot's list, not theirs.

Deleting is for someone added by mistake. It removes them from the depot but never from a record they signed: a check copies the name onto itself when it is submitted, so past checks read the same afterwards.

Suspend is the one to use for somebody who has left.

One depot, two roles: inspectors check vehicles, managers approve. Everyone here sees the same fleet.

An approval is a manager's claim that they reviewed the spot-check — the photos, the readings, the defect notes — and stand behind it. An inspector can't countersign their own check; the signing manager has to be someone else.
Keeping the depot safe

Everything you enter is kept on this phone only — there is no account and no server. Save a backup regularly: it writes the whole depot as one .json file to your downloads, and it is the only copy that exists off the phone.

The backup carries every vehicle, person, check and defect, plus your checklist. It does not carry the photos — the file records that a shot was taken, but restoring it on another phone shows those slots empty.

If the phone has not granted durable storage, the browser may clear the depot when the device runs short on space. Add the app to your home screen and it is usually granted; until then, back up often.

Add the app to your home screen and it opens with no signal — the whole shell is cached, so a spot-check in a basement car park or a dead spot at the back of the yard works the same as anywhere else.

Installing is also what usually earns the depot durable storage, so it is worth doing on day one rather than the day you lose a shift's checks.
